# Peaceful World — Transparency

Peaceful World treats transparency as a practical discipline: people should be able to understand what we are trying to build, how priorities change, how to participate and where official organizational information lives.

Transparency does **not** mean publishing every internal file, credential, unfinished experiment or private development repository.

## What we aim to make public

- our mission and public principles;
- current projects and high-level priorities;
- meaningful product and infrastructure updates;
- public contribution opportunities;
- legal and organizational information;
- funding and support channels;
- research sources where publication permits;
- public data provenance where data projects are involved.

## What remains private when appropriate

- credentials, signing keys and security-sensitive configuration;
- personal information;
- internal drafts that are not ready to represent the organization;
- release infrastructure where publication would increase risk;
- private collaborator or user information;
- material restricted by license, contract or data-protection obligations.

## GitHub transparency layer

GitHub is used to show the operational side of our work:

- [Public roadmap](ROADMAP.md)
- [Projects overview](PROJECTS.md)
- [Volunteer pathways](VOLUNTEER.md)
- public issues and contribution tasks;
- public repositories and releases where appropriate.

Some implementation repositories are private. Public-facing documentation should make the state of a project understandable even when its internal repository is not visible.

## Corrections

If you find an error in public technical or organizational documentation, please open an issue where possible or contact us at [peaceful-world.org/contacts](https://peaceful-world.org/contacts).
